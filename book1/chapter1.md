## Chapter One: The Boy Who Lived

Mr and Mrs Dursley of number four, Privet Drive, were proud to consider themselves perfectly ordinary people, and they had spent many years doing their best to avoid anything which might suggest otherwise. Mr Dursley sold drills and was a large man with a great deal of moustache and very little neck. Mrs Dursley was thin, fair-haired and exceptionally skilled at observing the affairs of neighbours who had not invited her to do so. Their son Dudley was, according to them, the finest boy in Surrey and possibly anywhere else besides.

There was only one subject capable of spoiling this comfortable picture of normality. Mrs Dursley had a sister named Lily Potter. Lily and her husband James were people whom the Dursleys preferred not to discuss, and they had a young son named Harry whom Vernon and Petunia had never met. This arrangement suited the Dursleys perfectly until one peculiar day when owls flew through the morning sky, strangers in cloaks gathered on street corners and Vernon Dursley repeatedly heard the name Potter whispered by people who appeared far too pleased with themselves.

Late that night, after every curtain on Privet Drive had been drawn, an old man appeared silently beside number four. Albus Dumbledore was tall and thin, with silver hair and a beard long enough to reach his belt. His blue eyes were normally bright behind half-moon spectacles, but there was little brightness in them that night. A tabby cat sat on the garden wall nearby. "Good evening, Professor McGonagall," said Dumbledore. The cat vanished and a severe-looking woman appeared in its place. She had spent most of the day watching the Dursleys and had formed an extremely poor opinion of them.

"So it is true?" she asked. Dumbledore nodded. James and Lily Potter were dead. Voldemort had disappeared. Their son had somehow survived. McGonagall looked sharply toward number four when Dumbledore told her what he intended to do. "You cannot mean to leave him here." Dumbledore replied that Harry had no closer family. "Half the wizarding world would take him," said McGonagall. "That," Dumbledore answered quietly, "is precisely the problem."

Before she could argue further, the roar of a motorcycle sounded overhead. Hagrid descended from the darkness and landed heavily in the road, carrying a bundle of blankets in his enormous arms. Inside was a sleeping baby with untidy black hair and a fresh cut upon his forehead shaped like a bolt of lightning. Hagrid's face was wet with tears. Dumbledore took Harry from him, carried the child through the gate and placed him carefully upon the doorstep. He tucked a letter into the blankets while McGonagall watched with tight lips and Hagrid attempted unsuccessfully to stop crying.

For a little while they stood looking down at Harry Potter. Across Britain, witches and wizards were already raising glasses to his name, though he knew nothing of Voldemort, nothing of the parents he had lost and nothing of the strange scar upon his forehead. Before dawn he would belong, as far as the ordinary world was concerned, to the Dursleys.

But number four, Privet Drive was not the only house in which people remained awake that night.

Far away, beyond wrought-iron gates and dark acres of ancient woodland, lights were burning in Malfoy Manor. Lucius Malfoy stood in his study feeding parchment into the fire. Letters vanished first, then names, then several objects which had become considerably more dangerous to possess than they had been the previous evening. Narcissa Malfoy entered carrying their infant daughter against her shoulder. Draconia had woken and was watching the flames with pale blue eyes.

"You have heard?" Narcissa asked. Lucius nodded. The Dark Lord had vanished while attacking the Potters. James and Lily were dead. Their son was not. "He survived the Killing Curse," Lucius said, and even he could not quite conceal his disbelief. Narcissa looked down at Draconia and held her a little more tightly.

"The Ministry will come," Lucius continued. "Tomorrow, perhaps sooner." Narcissa watched another letter disappear into the fire. "And what will you tell them?" Lucius looked at her. "That I was under the Imperius Curse." Neither of them bothered pretending she believed him.

Outside the windows, fireworks had begun to appear beyond the distant trees. Draconia reached toward the coloured flashes, fascinated. Narcissa caught her hand gently. "Whatever happens now, she stays out of this." Lucius answered at once. "Of course." Narcissa's expression did not change. "I mean it, Lucius." This time he was slower to reply.

Thousands of people were celebrating Voldemort's disappearance that night. Harry Potter slept on a doorstep beneath borrowed blankets. Draconia Malfoid slept beneath silk in one of the oldest wizarding houses in Britain. Harry had lost almost everything before he was old enough to understand what loss meant. Draconia had been born possessing nearly everything a child could be given.

Neither of them knew that the world had already begun deciding what they ought to become.
